# Not for sale

This repository is a study. It is a reading of proof of work next to public engineering at SpaceX, Tesla, and the companies around them. It does not claim that any of those companies uses this. A note on a page is not a trial and not a result.

Proof of work is a product. Miners make the blocks. Developers maintain the software. There is no vendor and no contract. Anyone may use the infrastructure. Nothing in this repository is for sale. A person or a company decides whether that use has value. The pages below are notes on tools that can be sequenced, each for its own reason.

It cannot phone a Kaspa office and have the chain switched off. There is no such office.

The companies stay under the laws of the places they operate. A license, a sanction, an export rule, or an order served on the company still binds them. The study looks at a hash, a covenant, and a KAS payment, and it writes down where those do not move a license, a beam, or a vehicle. The long record is the [conclusion](prompts/challenge-bot-build/conclusion.md). The short list a later pass would start from, if there is a later pass, is [From here](FROM-HERE.md). Neither page is a claim.

The driver is Nakamoto consensus. Miners order the blocks. No office can phone the chain off. The author called that cyberpunk, in that plain sense. A dollar unit is interesting only when it has the same property: no company key that can freeze it after it has moved. ## Part 1. No stablecoin

**What.** A public clock and a KAS lock, set next to the companies under the SpaceX and Tesla umbrella. The fifteen branch pages are that reading. The interesting matches are a hash of a file that is already fit to publish, and a KAS payment where both sides want KAS. Launch notes, a supplier pack, a charge receipt, a battery extract, a public status. Each page also says where the match stops.

**Why.** A company can be ordered. A bank, a cloud, or an app store can cut it off, because those switches have an office. The coin in this part has no issuer who mints it or freezes the coins that never entered a script. That is the property under test. It is not a claim that the company keeps operating, and it is not a claim that the company uses any of this.

**How.** The seal is 81 bytes. Version 1 is the hash the node commits to. KIP-21, marked Active on the rail page, commits accepted transactions by subnetwork. The native subnetwork is enough until a prover follows a lane. The example payment is `seal_escrow.sil`: an arbiter signature, a document hash, and an amount taken from that input alone. The script names 1000 sompi. The relay rule is a mass rate, not that number. A default node shows the transaction while the block is still at or after its pruning point, 108_000 seconds in rusty-kaspa v2.1.0. After that, a kept copy or a node that kept history. The file is not in the 81 bytes.

**Conclusion.** This part is interesting as a clock and as a KAS lock, and dull as a control system. It does not keep a launch license, a Starlink cell, a vehicle command, a contactor, a training run, or a medical file. Those stay on their own machines. The branch pages are the map of that line, company by company. Nothing in them was tried on a vehicle, a ship, or a factory.

## Part 2. A dollar design, not a coin

**What.** The full reading is [DOLLAR.md](DOLLAR.md). [Kaspa Dollar](https://kaspadollar.com/how-it-works) is a published design for a token minted against KAS locked in a covenant on the same base layer. The fifteen branch pages each apply that draft to one dollar surface. The roadmap page says the work is led by Alexander Safstrom, described there as a Kaspa core developer and the author of KIP-16, with support from the rest of that core team. The how-it-works page uses KSD as a reference label and says the ticker is not final. It is not in the v2.1.0 node. It is not a KIP. This repository does not hold its code. The same page says the proofs will be in a whitepaper before the fundraiser ends. That paper was not on the site on 6 November 2026. The site is also a fundraiser. This study does not ask anyone to send KAS, and it does not repeat the address.

**Why.** Part 1 can settle only in KAS. A purchase order written in dollars stays in dollars. A bank stablecoin has an issuer who can freeze the token. The published design tries to put a dollar unit in a vault of KAS, with the price coming from miner votes rather than from a company. If that design ever matches its own page, a covenant could aim at one dollar without a bank key. The collateral would still be KAS. A large fall can leave some units unbacked. The page says the protocol does not mint a new asset to spread that loss, and does not move it onto other vaults. DAGKnight does not do this job. KIP-2 is still Proposed and is not in v2.1.0. If it lands, a client can treat a block as ordered sooner. The dollar page posts its price through KIP-21 coinbase payloads and a proof, not through DAGKnight.

**How.** As the how-it-works page states it, and as that page warns that constants may change. Minting wants the locked KAS to be worth at least 2.5 times the units minted, so a new position can absorb a 60 percent fall before the debt exceeds the collateral. Below 200 percent, anyone can repay part of the debt and take KAS plus a penalty, which the page illustrates as the point where the price has fallen 20 percent from a full vault. Redeeming one unit takes one dollar of KAS from a vault the holder chooses. Miners may put a price in the coinbase payload. A ten-minute window counts only if at least half of its blocks voted. The median of those votes is the price. Moving that median past the honest votes takes at least half of the votes. The mint price may rise by at most 10 percent an hour. The liquidation price may fall by at most 25 percent an hour. A zero-knowledge proof is what the page says carries a valid window onto the chain, checked against the KIP-21 commitment. If no valid price is posted for 30 minutes, minting, withdrawing, liquidating, and redeeming stop. Adding KAS, repaying, and closing do not need a price. If no valid price is posted for 30 days, the page says anyone can settle a vault at the last price and end minting for that vault. The roadmap aims at a testnet in May 2027 and a mainnet in September 2027, after a fundraiser that closes on 1 January 2027. On 6 November 2026 the front page showed a little over 10 percent of a 5,000,000 KAS target. That figure moves. It is not a measure of whether the design works.

**Conclusion.** This part is the payment sentence of part 1, rewritten as a draft dollar with no company key, and only that sentence. When that unit exists, the transfer fee is the Kaspa mass rate, not a percentage of the amount. A card grows with the invoice. An in-app transfer that a product already calls free is not the fee this path undercuts. It would not keep a license, a beam, or a car. The oracle is the Nakamoto piece: a median of miner votes, not a foundation. It is still a draft. The whitepaper was not there to check, and the page says the constants may change. The branch pages now carry that draft. They do not rewrite a contract, and they do not treat the reference ticker as something `seal_escrow.sil` already locks.
